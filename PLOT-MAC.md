## DECISION M-A → the user: how the Mac app shares the iOS code — **DECIDED: B** (m2), Optimize for Mac + a Mac menu bar, macOS 14+, Apple silicon, App Sandbox on

The fact that decides most of it: the iOS app is built on UIKit through and through (73 files import it, ~1,000 uses, no platform switches anywhere), and `ConversationStore`, the hub every screen uses, creates CallKit/WebRTC/LiveKit objects and draws with UIKit. And tend-Mac may never edit `ios/`.

**A. A native SwiftUI macOS app in `mac/`, compiling `ios/Lime` sources by reference.**
*Pro:* the most Mac-like result (AppKit text editing, real Mac controls, Apple's long-term direction); no iPad-isms. *Con:* only the UIKit-free files compile (models, auth, network, storage: roughly 15% of the app). Everything else, including `ConversationStore`, every screen and the theme, either needs **hundreds of `#if os(macOS)` edits in `ios/`** (each an ask to plot-iOS, done by tend-iOS, risk to the phones) or is **rewritten in `mac/`** as a third UI (like Android, but in Swift). Months before parity; a second copy of the app's store logic = new "Mac debt".

**B. Mac Catalyst, from a separate project in `mac/` that compiles `ios/Lime` by reference.** `mac/project.yml` (XcodeGen, like iOS's) defines its own target with Catalyst on ("Optimize for Mac": real Mac-sized text and controls, not the 77% iPad scaling), pulls every `ios/Lime` file by path except the iOS entry point (`LimeApp.swift`) and the generated files, and adds Mac-only files in `mac/` (its own entry point, a two-pane window, menus, keyboard shortcuts). *Pro:* **the whole app compiles on the Mac on day one, unchanged** (UIKit exists under Catalyst), so chats, groups, jam, bands, settings and themes come over nearly free, and every iOS fix reaches the Mac at the next sync; **tend-Mac never edits `ios/`**: an Apple API missing on the Mac is a one-line guard asked of plot-iOS (or a small stand-in in `mac/` until then); Apple's own Messages app on the Mac is a Catalyst app. *Con:* some things feel iPad-ish until polished (text editing, context menus, toolbars); a few phone-only pieces need guards (CallKit, the camera picker, likely Bluetooth background modes), found by 603's first build; it is "the iOS app on a Mac", so Mac polish is ongoing work, not free.

**C. A multiplatform (or Catalyst) target inside the iOS project (`ios/project.yml`).**
*Pro:* technically the smallest switch (Catalyst on the existing target is a couple of settings). *Con:* **breaks the lanes**: every Mac change becomes an edit to `ios/`, i.e. tend-iOS work, and the Mac stops being a parallel track. A native macOS target here has A's compile problem as well. Only sensible if you decide one tend should own both.

**Not an option:** running the iPhone app on an Apple-silicon Mac ("Designed for iPhone"): free, but phone-sized, no side-by-side window, no menus.

**My lean: B.** It is the only option that gives you a Mac app with the list and the chat side by side soon, keeps tend-Mac out of `ios/`, and doesn't create a third copy of the app's logic. If Catalyst ever limits us (most likely: the chat composer's text editing), we can replace single screens with Mac-native ones inside `mac/` without changing the decision. **Plot's calls inside B (change any):** "Optimize for Mac"; macOS 14 Sonoma minimum (it matches iOS 17); Apple-silicon only for now (Intel before shipping, if you want it); App Sandbox on from the first build (the Mac App Store needs it). Your call.

## DESIGN-M1: linking a device to your phone — **D1–D5 DECIDED (m2): D1 yes, D2 yes, D3 45 days, D4 server, D5 up to 5 devices. D6 OPEN (below).**

**The user's principle (m2): it must "just work", like Apple's Messages.** A linked device stays signed in for as long as it's used: sessions refresh silently, no periodic re-sign-in, no sign-out except Unlink or Sign out on that device, and adding a device never signs another one out. Linking covers **every kind of device**: computers (Mac, PC), an iPad, and a second phone (e.g. an Android beside the iPhone). Up to **5 devices per account**, any mix. Wherever the text below says "computer", read "new device".

Applies to the Mac now and to Windows, the iPad and Android. It changes `core/` and the server, so after you approve it, plot-iOS and plot-Mac split the work: **most of it is phone-side (iOS + core)**, the Mac side is the QR screen and receiving.

**What exists today (surveyed):** your account has one **master key**; its secret lives only on your phone. Every device has its own keys, signed by the master key, and people's apps send each message to every device the master key vouches for. The server checks the signature but never holds the master secret. Moving to a new phone (Move) already pairs two devices by QR with a 6-digit check code and an encrypted stream.

### 1. How linking works (lean)
1. **On the computer:** you sign in as usual (email or username, password, the emailed code). The computer does **not** register yet (registering on its own would replace your keys and sign your phone out, so core forbids it on a computer). It shows a **QR code**: its own new device keys plus a one-time secret, valid 5 minutes. For anyone who can't use the camera: a short code to type on the phone instead, followed by a 6-digit match check on both screens.
2. **On the phone:** Settings → Linked Devices → **Link a computer** → Face ID / passcode (always, even with Lock lime off) → scan. The phone **signs the computer's keys with the master key** and registers it (the server's existing rule: same master key = nothing else changes). The master secret never leaves the phone.
3. The phone sends the computer a **link package**, end-to-end encrypted to the computer's new keys and sealed with the QR's one-time secret (so only the computer you scanned can open it, even if someone tampered with the server): your profile, contacts and their verified keys, chat and group list, blocked people, jam subscriptions and band keys, settings that sync, and the history you chose (§3).
4. Both screens say what happened: the computer "Linked to Shem's iPhone"; the phone adds "MacBook" to Linked Devices and a one-time note in Settings: "A computer was linked on Nov 14".
- **D1 (your call): does the computer need your password + emailed code as well as the phone?** *Lean: yes* (three things: password, inbox, phone in hand; needs no new server code). Alternative: QR only (WhatsApp-style, easier) needs a new server function that mints a session for the computer.

### 2. Keys and the app lock on the computer (lean)
- The computer's device keys live in its encrypted store; the store key in the **Mac Keychain, this-Mac-only, never iCloud**, wrapped by the **Secure Enclave** where the Mac has one (Windows later: TPM / DPAPI).
- The computer **can't**: link other devices, change keys, move the account or delete it (phone-only, by design).
- **D2: Lock lime is ON by default on a computer** (*lean: yes*; computers are shared in staff rooms and at home): Touch ID or the Mac password, locks when the Mac sleeps or locks and after 5 minutes away (changeable); the window is blank while locked. Notifications show the sender's name only by default.

### 3. What history comes over: D3 (your call)
- **H1 Start fresh:** only messages that arrive after linking. *Pro:* nothing old copied, simplest. *Con:* chats look empty; no search of the past at your desk.
- **H2 Recent messages (lean):** the phone asks "Bring your last 45 days of messages to this computer?" with two buttons, **Bring recent messages** / **Start fresh**. Text and small media come over; big attachments come over only while the server still has them, else they say "On your phone". *Pro:* useful at once; one-time; you choose each time. *Con:* more to build; a bigger copy exists on the computer.
- **H3 Everything:** *Con:* slow and large; the most exposed if a computer is lost.
- **D4: how the history travels.** *Lean:* one encrypted file through the existing attachment upload, deleted after download or 24 h (works on school Wi-Fi that blocks device-to-device). Alternative: direct over local Wi-Fi like Move (the server sees nothing, but often fails at school).

### 4. Staying in sync after linking (core work, both apps)
Messages you send from the phone appear on the computer and vice versa (copies to your own devices, encrypted like any message); read state, edits, deletes, reactions, Notes to self and blocks follow. Group chats share their keys with the new device for messages from now on. (Plot to verify what core already does here before any brief.)

### 5. Unlinking and lost computers
- Phone → Linked Devices → a computer → **Unlink**: the server stops delivering to it (a new small server function, the only one in this design), group keys rotate so it can't read new group messages, and the computer wipes itself the next time it opens. The computer's own **Sign out** = unlink + wipe.
- A lost computer: unlink from the phone. Honest limit: what was already on its disk stays there, encrypted, behind the Mac's login and Lock lime.
- Moving to a new phone or resetting keys unlinks every computer (they link again).
- **D5 (decided):** at most **5 devices** per account, any mix (the phone included). Adding a sixth asks you to unlink one first.

### 6. What each party learns
- **lime's server:** one more device row for your account (id, public keys, the master signature, when it was added; **no device name**: names travel only inside encryption); messages to you are now delivered to two devices, so it sees two mailbox items per message (it already sees how many devices you have); the computer's IP address when it connects (as for phones); for H2, one encrypted file of a padded size and when it was uploaded and fetched; for Unlink, when a device was revoked. **No new tables except the revoke time on the existing row; nothing logged with ids or IPs.**
- **Your contacts:** their apps see one more device for you (they encrypt to it). Your safety number does **not** change (same master key), and **they get no notice** (*lean*; Signal and WhatsApp don't either).
- **Apple:** an encrypted Keychain item on the Mac; Touch ID stays on the Mac; later, push through APNs (DESIGN-37 covers it).
- **The phone:** the computer's keys and name. **The computer:** everything in your account from linking on, plus the history you chose.
- **Abuse case:** someone with your unlocked phone for a minute links their laptop. Guards: Face ID/passcode at the scan, your password and inbox on the laptop, the Linked Devices list and the one-time note on the phone.

**Answered (m2):** D1 yes · D2 yes · D3 the last 45 days · D4 one encrypted file through the server · D5 up to 5 devices.

### 7. D6 → **DECIDED: K2 (the user, 2026-11-14, m3)**: every phone and tablet holds the master key (iPhone, Android, iPad); computers never do. Any phone or tablet approves a new device; losing one is fixed by unlinking it from another; losing all of them falls back to today's reset.
Today the master key's secret lives on **one** device (the first phone). With five devices, that means: only that phone can link anything, and **if it's lost or broken, the account's keys reset**: every linked device is signed out and every contact sees "security key changed". That isn't "it just works".
- **K1. Only the first phone holds it (today).** *Pro:* smallest risk surface. *Con:* the phone is a single point of failure; you can't link a new device from your iPad or Mac.
- **K2. Every phone and tablet holds it (iPhone, Android, iPad); computers don't.** Any phone or tablet can approve a new device; losing one phone changes nothing (unlink it from another). *Pro:* close to Messages; computers, the most shared and most exposed, stay unable to add devices. *Con:* the key exists on up to three devices; a stolen, unlocked phone could link a device (guards: Face ID/passcode at approval, your password and inbox on the new device, a note on all your devices).
- **K3. Every device holds it.** *Pro:* simplest mental model; any device approves any device. *Con:* a shared or stolen computer can add devices.
- **My lean: K2.** And losing *all* phones and tablets falls back to today's reset (sign in again, contacts see a key change), which is honest and rare.
