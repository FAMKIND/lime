## 1. Gallup, 2022: K-12 burnout
*K-12 Workers Have Highest Burnout Rate in U.S.*, Stephanie Marken and Sangeeta Agrawal, Gallup, June 13, 2022. https://news.gallup.com/poll/393500/workers-highest-burnout-rate.aspx

- "More than four in 10 K-12 workers in the U.S. (44%) say they "always" or "very often" feel burned out at work, outpacing all other industries nationally." (first paragraph) → page: **44%**
- "Within the K-12 employee population, teachers are the most burned out, at 52%." (the paragraph after the survey details) → page: **52%**
- "...conducted Feb. 3-14, 2022, with 12,319 U.S. full-time employees, including 1,263 K-12 workers." → page: **February 2022**

## 2. RAND, 2024: teacher well-being
*Teacher Well-Being and Intentions to Leave in 2024: Findings from the 2024 State of the American Teacher Survey*, Sy Doan, Elizabeth D. Steiner, Rakesh Pandey, RAND, published June 18, 2024. https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA1108-12.html

- "...compared with comparable working adults, about twice as many teachers reported experiencing frequent job-related stress or burnout and roughly three times as many teachers reported difficulty coping with job-related stress." (Key Takeaways, first bullet; the survey was in January 2024) → page: **about twice as many**

## 3. Learning Policy Institute, 2017: turnover
*Teacher Turnover: Why It Matters and What We Can Do About It*, Desiree Carver-Thomas and Linda Darling-Hammond, Learning Policy Institute, August 16, 2017. https://learningpolicyinstitute.org/product/teacher-turnover-report

- "...this report finds that 90% of open teaching positions are created by teachers who leave the profession." (summary, first paragraph) → page: **90%**

## 4. Ronfeldt, Loeb and Wyckoff, 2013: turnover and students
*How Teacher Turnover Harms Student Achievement*, Matthew Ronfeldt, Susanna Loeb and James Wyckoff, *American Educational Research Journal* 50(1), 4–36, February 2013. https://doi.org/10.3102/0002831212463813 (abstract, via Crossref)

- "...this study estimates the effects of teacher turnover on over 850,000 New York City fourth- and fifth-grade student observations over 8 years. The results indicate that students in grade levels with higher turnover score lower in both English language arts (ELA) and math..." → page: **850,000 New York City student observations over 8 years; lower in English language arts and math**

## 5. Ronfeldt, Farmer, McQueen and Grissom, 2015: collaboration
*Teacher Collaboration in Instructional Teams and Student Achievement*, Matthew Ronfeldt, Susanna Owens Farmer, Kiel McQueen and Jason A. Grissom, *American Educational Research Journal* 52(3), 475–514, June 2015. https://doi.org/10.3102/0002831215585562 (abstract, via Crossref)

- "This study draws upon survey and administrative data on over 9,000 teachers in 336 Miami-Dade County public schools over 2 years..." → page: **over 9,000 teachers, 336 Miami-Dade schools**
- "Teachers and schools that engage in better quality collaboration have better achievement gains in math and reading. Moreover, teachers improve at greater rates when they work in schools with better collaboration quality." → page: **better-quality collaboration, better gains in math and reading; teachers improve at greater rates**
